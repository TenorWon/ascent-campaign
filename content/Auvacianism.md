### Formation

Nobody knows exactly how it started. Some say an ancestor of [[Noleis]] may have been contacted by the divine being Auvac Themself, while others theorize that the creator god's hypothetical hand could have been forced by mortals who became almost too interested in the nature of [[Larentum]]'s origins. Regardless of how it originated, the galaxy's largest and most influential religion has touched the lives of trillions throughout history.

Today, Auvac worship is integral to a great many populations. Not only is it the founding principle of the modern [[The Kingdom of the Sun|Kingdom of the Sun]], but it also serves as a guidepost in cultures on home worlds like [[Boullaris]], [[Alagrii]], and [[Mesokarr]].

Auvacian culture is incredibly rich due to its deep historical roots. Anthropologists and theologists say that evidence found across the galaxy suggests that the religion has been around since the 1500s. Since its establishment in the wider schema of the galaxy, it has expanded upon and modernized its customs.

### Worship

The weekly celebration of witness it older than most other traditions known today. Like clockwork, Auvac's followers find the time in their day to visit a sanctum, an established place of worship, to join in prayer and give thanks. This event normally takes about an hour and is led by at least one clergy member called an oracle. One can expect a brief offering of song, followed by a lecture from the elder oracle, followed by what is often referred to as the "period of witness." This is a short time taken at the end of a witness ceremony when those who are able look upward, as if to make direct eye contact with Auvac. If one finds themself in one of the more traditional sanctums, this period will likely involve looking into the sun for some amount of time—usually as long as one can stand.

### Auvac and Mortals

The consensus is that Auvac is a creator god. What They want with Their creation is not set in stone, but is largely agreed upon that They wish only to serve as a powerful benefactor to Their people. Some theorize on the form of Auvac, but this is normally frowned upon and seen as offensive in the religion, as the perspective is that we mortals are too small to even claim to ponder Auvac's being or form. To do so would be to hypothesize Their limits, Their weaknesses, and Their misfortunes, all of which do not exist.

Auvac Themself does not speak—nor do they send profits or prophecies. Instead, an oracle is ritualistically given the power to channel Their voice, after which process they become a Willbearer. This ritual is said to be extremely taxing, sometimes causing one to be bedridden for days before they can perform their duties. This religious leader serves at the side of the Sunbound Monarch, providing the presence of the creator god at pivotal moments and allowing Them to speak when They deem necessary.

Through connection with Auvac, the Sunbound Monarch leads nations with religious zeal and decisive action. In a ritualistic process still held secret to all of the galaxy after millennia, it is said that the monarch's fate is connected directly to the sun above [[Mesokarr]], where all of the Kingdom of the Sun's past leaders have gone after death. This connection is meant to allow the royal figure to commune with their predecessors, and supposedly to enhance their constitution through supernatural means.

No other mortals are so lucky as to undergo these exclusive rituals, but all worshippers of Auvac are of the belief that they will find life after death in one of Auvac's many suns, the first of Their creations in the cosmos—meant to light the way for the living, and to hold a place for those passed on. It is said that the dead become the rays of the suns, and that if one of their living loved ones passed beneath the light of their sun, a glow of warm sunlight may appear to guide that person to the place they most need to be.
