### Culture

The explosion of [[Stalvrun]]'s core is a pre-[[Larentum#Galactization|galactization]] event that is still being studied to this day, as the reasons behind remain unknown. What is known, though, is that is forced the Stalvraks to spread to the various astral bodies in their solar system. Their culture is unified by the one trauma they all share, as well as the fact that their system is a member of the [[The Galactic Union|Galactic Union]]. However from there, they differ greatly.

There are 7 planets and moons that used to orbit Stalvrun. They have since begun to fall into a new orbit, which is thus far predicted to be a safely aligned orbit around the largest planet, now designated [[New Stalvrun]]. As there is only a limited number of senators that can represent a species or nation in the [[Union Senate]], this is the planet that sends representatives.

Stalvraks are known to be somewhat paranoid, stereotypically, despite their inherent adaptations to resist hardship. They adapt easily to modern technology, and many actually enjoy hunting for sport with modern-day weaponry as a celebration of their mastery over "fate," or as a celebration of their continued survival. Many grow up knowing how lucky they are that they were not there to experience the core's explosion, and that their ancestors survived so that they could be here today.

### Biology

Pre-historic Stalvraks were all of similar skin, eye, and hair color, hair texture, etc. Since Stalvrun's explosion, the differing environments on the 7 surrounding planets and moons has changed the species into one with 7 different varying patterns. This also applies to the stripes that line either side of their torsos. However, one thing all Stalvraks have in common is their extreme readiness. The venomous creatures on Stalvrun have given the people a resistance to harsh poisons over time. Additionally, living on a planet with such active geological activity caused the evolution of small tendrils on their bodies, almost like hair, which can sense vibrations in the ground.
